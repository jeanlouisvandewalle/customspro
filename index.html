II

AEO guidance

Fixed price — no surprises

Better customs processes, savings, more efficient goods flows, mutual trust and a competitive edge: that is what Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) status delivers.

The AEO authorisation shows that your company is a reliable link in the international supply chain. Customs grants priority treatment, and a growing number of customers and suppliers expect it from their partners. There are two types, which can be combined: AEO-C (customs simplifications) and AEO-S (security).

What we do

  • Self-assessment and gap analysis
  • Drafting written procedures and internal controls
  • Training the people involved
  • Support during the customs audit and the three-yearly re-audit
A common mistake: “It is only a questionnaire” or “One person will sort this out before year-end.” AEO calls for a supported, organisation-wide approach. Our experience with AEO projects keeps your organisation from being overloaded.

VII

Operations, authorisations & regulatory compliance

CBAM · EUDR · Anti-dumping · Excise

A steady stream of new trade legislation lands on the goods flows you already have. CBAM, the Deforestation Regulation, anti-dumping measures and excise rules each come with their own scope, evidence and reporting — and each of them can stop a shipment at the border if the obligation is discovered too late.

We review your operations and your existing authorisations against the legislation in force, establish which obligations genuinely apply to your goods, and build them into your processes and master data. The objective is simple: keep your logistics flows moving, with the regulatory work done before the goods are on the road.

What we review

  • CBAM: scope per CN code, embedded emissions data, reporting and certificates
  • EUDR: Annex I scoping, due diligence statements and supplier evidence
  • Anti-dumping and countervailing duties: scope, origin evidence and circumvention risk
  • Excise: authorisations, tax warehouses, EMCS movements and exemptions
  • Export controls and sanctions screening on the same flows
  • Product legislation and prohibitions that surface at import or export

Your authorisations

Authorisations age quietly. We check that yours still match what your business actually does — AEO, special procedures such as inward and outward processing, customs warehousing, simplified and centralised clearance, excise authorisations — and whether the conditions, guarantees and record-keeping behind them still hold up in an audit.

The result: one overview per goods flow of the obligations that apply, who owns each of them internally, and what has to be in place before the shipment moves — instead of a call from your forwarder at the border.

VIII

Customs documents & declarations

Drawn up by us

Advice is one thing; the document that accompanies the goods is another. CustomsPro also draws up the paperwork itself — with the classification, value and origin behind it checked before it is filed, not after a control.

What we prepare

  • Export and import declarations
  • One-sided Belgian export declarations for parties without their own set-up in Belgium
  • Regularisation declarations to put earlier movements right
  • Transit documents (T1, T2) and their discharge
  • Proofs of preferential origin: EUR.1, A.TR, REX statements on origin
  • Certificates of origin and supplier's declarations
  • Documents for special procedures: inward and outward processing, customs warehousing, temporary admission
  • Excise documents and EMCS movements

Each document is prepared from your own data — commercial invoice, product master, contracts and Incoterms — and the underlying positions are documented, so the file behind the declaration stands up years later. Where a flow repeats, we set it up once and keep it running.

Ad hoc or structural: a single urgent declaration, a batch of regularisations, or the standing preparation of your documents — with a fixed price per document type agreed up front.
